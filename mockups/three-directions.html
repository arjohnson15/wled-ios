Phone app — three directions

Mockups with made-up data, so nothing here talks to a controller. Each direction has four screens; scroll each row sideways. You can pick one direction, or mix pieces, e.g. A's tab bar with C's house photo. Interactive "Your house" →

Direction A

The remote

A proper app shell. There's a tab bar at the bottom and no hamburger menu. The lights strip stays pinned at the top while you scroll. Each tab does one thing (Lights, Looks, Colour, Schedule), so you never dig through eight tabs. Every control is in thumb reach. This is closest to what we have, rebuilt around a phone.

Direction B

Control Center

Borrowed from the iPhone's own Control Center and the Hue app. Brightness is one big vertical slider you drag with your thumb, and zones are big square tiles you tap on or off. Colour, looks and times slide up as sheets over the screen, so you never lose sight of the lights. It's the fewest screens and the fastest to use, and it hides the advanced parts.

Direction C

Your house

The traced photo of your house is the main screen, with the rooflines glowing in the colours they're actually showing. Tap a run on the picture to pick that zone, and a sheet slides up with its controls. This uses the House photos work we already have, and nobody else's app does it. It needs a traced photo, and falls back to direction A's screens without one.

My recommendation: C's house photo as the home screen when there is one, on A's app shell (bottom tab bar, Colour as its own screen, Looks with search and filters), with B's two best ideas borrowed: the vertical brightness slider and the timeline for schedules. All three work with the current cloud. None of them needs firmware changes, and the colour screen already streams to the lights as you drag. Tell me which you like, or which parts, and I'll build it.
